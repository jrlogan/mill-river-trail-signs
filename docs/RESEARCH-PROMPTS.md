# Deep research prompts

Prompts for a deep-research tool, aimed at the places this project is soft.
Written 26 August 2026. Keep them here so they are not retyped from memory, and
add to them as questions close.

**Two rounds of AI deep research have already been run** (see `DECISIONS.md`).
Both were useful and both had the same failure modes: a bibliography of links
that were not tied to individual claims, confident prose around unverified
details, and at least one citation that turned out to be a different person with
the same name. The preamble below exists to fight that. **Paste it in front of
every prompt.**

---

## The preamble — paste this first, every time

> Ground rules for this research. I am writing text that will be printed on
> metal interpretive signs on a public trail and left outdoors for twenty years.
> Being wrong is expensive and embarrassing, so I need to know exactly how
> confident to be about each thing you tell me.
>
> 1. **Footnote every factual claim individually** to the specific source it came
>    from — not a bibliography at the end. If a sentence has no source, say so in
>    the sentence.
> 2. **Grade each finding** as VERIFIED (you saw it in a primary or archival
>    source, or in two independent secondary ones), REPORTED (one secondary
>    source, uncorroborated), or UNVERIFIED (plausible, no source found).
> 3. **Negative findings are as valuable as positive ones.** If something does
>    not appear to exist, or the record contradicts a common claim, say that
>    plainly and tell me what you searched. Do not fill a gap with narrative.
> 4. **Watch for name and place collisions.** Distinguish Fair Haven,
>    Connecticut from Fair Haven, New Jersey; the Mill River in New Haven from
>    the Mill River in Fairfield County and the one in Massachusetts; the West
>    River from the Mill River; and the colonial "watch house" on the New Haven
>    Green from the 19th-century oyster watch houses.
> 5. **For every image or document you suggest**, give: the holding institution,
>    the collection and call or accession number if you can find one, the date,
>    the creator, and the **copyright or rights status**. Say if it is public
>    domain, and why (published before 1930, US government work, etc.).
> 6. **Prefer things I can actually get**: digitised collections, finding aids,
>    Chronicling America, HathiTrust, Internet Archive, NARA, the Connecticut
>    Digital Archive, the New Haven Museum's Whitney Library, Yale's Manuscripts
>    and Archives. Name the finding aid, not just the institution.
> 7. If a question turns on a document nobody has digitised, **tell me which
>    physical volume to ask for and at which desk.**

## 1. The tide gate — PARTLY ANSWERED, one thing still open

**Run this narrowed version, not the original.** The location is settled (State
Street bridge, fixed by Conn. Agencies Regs. § 26-108-1) and the 1930s
work-relief and mosquito-control context is established. What is still missing is
the year and the drawing.

> Two specific questions about the **Mill River tide gates at the State Street
> bridge in New Haven, Connecticut**.
>
> **One: the year.** A previous research pass gave 1935, built with WPA relief
> labour under the Connecticut Agricultural Experiment Station's mosquito
> programme, and cited a "CIRCA / UConn Infrastructure Inventory, Appendix D"
> that I cannot find — and the regional literature says no comprehensive New
> England tide gate inventory has ever been compiled. **Find the primary
> document, or tell me the 1935 date is unsupported.** Search Connecticut State
> Library **RG 099, CAES records, Series 3: Mosquito Control and Marsh
> Reclamation Files 1915-1945**, especially the New Haven and Hamden town
> inspection folders; the **City Year Book for the City of New Haven for fiscal
> years 1933-1938**, which carries the City Engineer's and Public Works annual
> reports; WPA and Federal Emergency Relief project listings for New Haven; and
> New Haven newspapers 1933-1938.
>
> **Two: the drawing.** Does the Olmsted Archives hold a plan titled something
> like *"East Rock Pk. / Mill River Dam / With Tide Gate, Sluices and High Level
> Spillway"*? It has been cited to Olmsted Brothers Job #05313, and elsewhere in
> the same source to "Job #03352 / Plan #05313," so at least one of those numbers
> is wrong. **Search Olmsted Online and the Olmsted Associates Records at the
> Library of Congress for the firm's New Haven jobs** and tell me what tide gate
> or Mill River dam material actually exists, with correct job and plan numbers
> and rights status. A pre-1930 Olmsted drawing of a tide gate designed for this
> river would be the image this sign needs.

## 1b. Original, longer version — superseded, kept for reference

Our sign 6 is about the Mill River tide gate. We cannot establish when it was
built, and "around 1930" has been repeated at us by two sources without a
citation behind either.

> When were the tide gates across the **Mill River in New Haven, Connecticut**
> — at what is now the Interstate 91 crossing, near State Street — actually
> built? I need a year with a document behind it.
>
> Context I already have: the 1910 Cass Gilbert and Frederick Law Olmsted plan
> for New Haven recommended a tide gate. New Haven ran an anti-mosquito marsh
> campaign in the 1910s that involved ditching, oiling and "looking after the
> tide gates" (The New Haven Union, 30 June 1912, p18). The city put tide gates
> across the **West River** in 1919 for mosquito control, and replaced those with
> three self-regulating gates in 2012 — **that is a different river and I do not
> want it conflated with the Mill.** Yale's water-management summary says the
> Mill River gates were "installed in the 1930s" without a citation.
>
> Please search specifically in:
> - New Haven **Board of Public Works** and **City Engineer** annual reports
> - the **Court of Common Council** journals of proceedings (these are printed
>   annually and recorded exactly this kind of contract in the 1880s, so the
>   series should continue)
> - the New Haven **Board of Health** and any mosquito-elimination commission
>   reports
> - **Army Corps of Engineers** New Haven Harbor navigation and improvement
>   documents
> - Connecticut DEEP or state highway department records, since I-91 was later
>   built over the site
> - New Haven newspapers 1915-1945, especially the New Haven Union, the New
>   Haven Register and the Journal-Courier
>
> Tell me the year, the authorising body, the cost if recorded, and who built it.
> If the answer is that the gates went in over several phases, say that. If you
> cannot find it, tell me exactly which volume and which repository I should be
> asking for in person.

## 2. Settle the Whitney dam, and the mills that named the river

Sign 4 currently follows the Eli Whitney Museum's own sign because it stands at
the dam, but that contradicts the trail's other main source, and we should not
print a contested number.

> I need two things about the **Mill River in New Haven and Hamden,
> Connecticut**.
>
> **First, settle a contradiction.** The Eli Whitney Museum's interpretive sign
> at the Whitney dam gives the dam as **38 feet tall and 500 feet long, opened
> 1862 and expanded twice since**. The Mill River Trail's own published history
> says the dam was **raised 6 feet to 27 feet, then to 31 feet, in 1860 and 1866,
> plus 19 inches in 1916**. These cannot both be right. Using New Haven Water
> Company records, engineering reports, state water-supply surveys, National
> Register documentation and period newspapers, tell me what the dam's actual
> dimensions and construction dates were, and where each figure originates.
>
> **Second, the mills.** I want to confirm or refute three things:
> - That New Haven Colony authorised a communal water-powered **grist mill in
>   1642** at the lower falls at the base of East Rock, that it was called simply
>   "the Mill" in the town records, and that this is why the river is called the
>   Mill River. Give me the actual town-record wording if it survives.
> - That the Connecticut General Assembly granted a **twenty-year monopoly in
>   1718** for a water-powered **linseed oil mill** in New Haven County, built at
>   the middle falls in what is now Hamden. I want the Assembly record.
> - The phrase **"thirteen mills north."** Is there a documented count of mill
>   seats on the Mill River between New Haven and Cheshire, and where does the
>   number thirteen come from? Name the seats, their dates and their trades.
>
> *The History of Hamden, Connecticut, 1786-1959* is on the Internet Archive and
> is probably the fastest route to the second and third of these.

## 3. Our two weakest signs: planning, and the highway that wasn't

Signs 9 and 10 have **five empty image slots between them and no leads at all**
after two rounds of research. They are the candidates to be deferred unless
something turns up.

> I need illustrations and hard detail for two interpretive signs about
> **20th-century planning and highway politics in New Haven, Connecticut**.
>
> **Sign 9 — a century of plans.** The 1910 Cass Gilbert and Frederick Law
> Olmsted civic improvement report; **Maurice Rotival's** plans for New Haven
> from the 1940s onward; and the Richard C. Lee redevelopment era. I want:
> published plan drawings, perspective renderings and maps that show the **Mill
> River corridor specifically**; where the originals are held; and their rights
> status. Yale holds Rotival material — name the collection and its finding aid.
>
> **Sign 10 — the highway that was stopped.** In the 1960s the state proposed a
> highway connector running along the Mill River and through East Rock Park.
> Residents formed a **Save Our Park Committee** and defeated it, though
> Interstate 91 still took about ten acres. I want:
> - The connector's official name, route, sponsoring agency and the dates it was
>   proposed and abandoned. Route maps and alignment drawings from Connecticut
>   Highway Department or Bureau of Public Roads files.
> - **Who the Save Our Park Committee actually were** — names, the organisation's
>   records if they survive, and any photographs of meetings, protests, petitions
>   or hearings. This is the kind of story that lives in a New Haven Register
>   photo morgue or somebody's basement.
> - Construction photographs of **I-91 being built through the Mill River
>   corridor and past East Rock**, 1960s.
> - Anything on **Rice Field** and the Olmsted Brothers' riverside portion of
>   East Rock Park, before and after the highway.
>
> For each image: institution, collection, accession number, date, rights.

## 4. William Lanson, and the stone that went down the river

Sign 13 is about William Lanson and has **only one image**. He is the most
consequential person on this trail and the most poorly illustrated.

> **William Lanson** — known as "King Lanson" — was a Black contractor and
> community leader in early 19th-century New Haven, Connecticut, who extended
> Long Wharf and built the Farmington Canal locks. I need three things.
>
> **Illustration.** No portrait is known to exist. So what *can* stand in? I want
> period views of **Long Wharf and his extension**, the **Farmington Canal**
> works, the **New Liberia / New Guinea** settlement he founded, the 2020
> memorial sculpture on the Farmington Canal Trail, and any surviving document in
> his own hand or bearing his name — deeds, contracts, court records,
> petitions. Institution, collection, accession number and rights for each.
>
> **The scows.** Local tradition on the Mill River says Lanson's crews quarried
> stone at East Rock and moved it **down the Mill River by flat-bottomed scow**
> to Long Wharf. Separately, the Eli Whitney Museum's own sign says charcoal came
> **up** the Mill River by scow to the armory forges, and that Whitney supplied
> **Ithiel Town** with the stone for **Trinity Church on the Green**. I want:
> - Documentary evidence for scow, gundalow, lighter or barge traffic on the Mill
>   River in the first half of the 19th century — what the vessels were called
>   locally, what they carried, and how far upriver they could get.
> - Evidence tying Lanson's quarrying and haulage to East Rock and to this river
>   specifically rather than to the harbour generally.
> - **Any image** of a stone scow, quarry scow or similar working craft in New
>   Haven Harbour or on Connecticut rivers before 1930. I have found nothing.
>   Goode's *Fisheries and Fishery Industries of the United States* (1887) and
>   Ingersoll's *The Oyster Industry* (1881) both have New Haven chapters and
>   plates — check those first.
>
> **The record.** Where are the primary sources on Lanson — New Haven land
> records, the Farmington Canal Company papers, court files, and the 19th-century
> pamphlet attacks on him? Name the volumes.

## 5. Verification sweep — the loose leads

These are the things sitting in our notes as REPORTED or UNVERIFIED. Each one is
a sentence away from a sign, and each one could be wrong.

> Please verify or refute each of the following about **New Haven, Connecticut**.
> Grade each one, cite each one individually, and tell me what you searched even
> where you find nothing. Short answers are fine; I want confidence levels more
> than prose.
>
> 1. **Talmadge Bros.** oyster company operating on the **Mill River** — said to
>    be on the east bank south of English Station, with shell piles and equipment
>    surviving. The New Haven Museum's own River Street history names Bigelow,
>    National Pipe Bending, a Ford assembly plant, an A&P warehouse and the
>    Quinnipiac Brewery, and **no oyster firm**. Was Talmadge ever on the Mill
>    River, or is this a misremembering of their Norwalk operation?
> 2. **A trolley turntable near the Mill River District** — the block around
>    James Street, State Street and Grand Avenue. The Connecticut Company's James
>    Street car barn (c. 1920) and the Grand Avenue power station and repair shops
>    are established. **Was there a turntable, and where?** Sanborn fire insurance
>    maps should settle this one way or the other.
> 3. **The midnight oyster derby** — the claim that hundreds of skiffs and
>    sharpies lined up on the Quinnipiac and Mill River flats and raced out when
>    the church bells struck midnight on the opening of the season. Real
>    tradition, or embellishment? New Haven newspapers for late August and
>    September, 1860-1900.
> 4. **Yale crews rowing on the Mill River** tidal reach and Lake Whitney in the
>    mid-1800s, and any role in the origins of American collegiate rowing. Yale
>    athletics records and the New Haven papers.
> 5. **Milton J. Stewart of East Rock.** He owned the summit, built oyster boats
>    on it and sledged them down over snow — that much I have from the Morning
>    Journal and Courier, 22 February 1887. Three things I cannot confirm: **(a)**
>    that he prophesied Long Island Sound would flood Connecticut and built the
>    boat as an ark, a story told only by the Brooklyn *Daily Standard Union* on
>    4 September 1899; **(b)** the counterfeiting workings said to have been found
>    under his stone house when it was demolished in 1884; **(c)** the roughly
>    $40,000 condemnation award and his death as a pauper. **And above all: does a
>    photograph or engraving of the boat on the summit exist anywhere?**
> 6. **The Oyster Shell Field.** New Haven's Whitney Library holds "Plan of ye
>    lotts in the oyster shell field so called," 1750, Map G3784.N4P527 1750g,
>    from the Town of New Haven Proprietors records v.2. **Where exactly was the
>    Oyster Shell Field**, in terms of present-day streets and its relation to the
>    Mill River? Is the name from Indigenous shell middens, colonial dumping, or
>    both? Has the map been digitised?
> 7. **Max Dellfant (1867-1943)**, maritime painter, Quinnipiac Avenue, studio in
>    the Mansfield Oyster Company loft. The New Haven Museum holds MSS #276, the
>    Dellfant-Ross correspondence. **Do any Dellfant paintings of oyster watch
>    houses or the working waterfront survive in public collections, and are any
>    reproducible?**

## 6. Species, walked

The weakest thing on the finished signs, and the easiest to fix badly.

> Our interpretive signs each carry a four-species panel — plants and animals a
> visitor could actually notice at that spot, deliberately mixing natives with
> introduced and invasive species, because knowing which is which is the point.
> **Our current picks are educated guesses made from a desk** and need replacing
> with what is actually recorded there.
>
> For the **lower Mill River corridor in New Haven, Connecticut** — from the
> Whitney dam down through East Rock Park, past the tide gate at I-91, to the
> tidal reach at Criscuolo Park and the harbour — tell me what is actually
> documented, section by section:
> - **iNaturalist** research-grade observations, and **eBird** hotspot data for
>   East Rock Park, Rice Field and Criscuolo Park, with the commonest species and
>   their seasons.
> - Plant surveys in the **Save the Sound Mill River watershed plan**, CT DEEP
>   natural diversity data, and any published vegetation survey of the salt marsh
>   remnants.
> - **Which invasives are the real management problem here** — I expect
>   Phragmites, Japanese knotweed, garlic mustard, Japanese barberry, mugwort —
>   and where each is worst along this river.
> - The state of the **river herring run**: current alewife and blueback counts
>   for the Mill River, fishway status, and what a visitor might realistically see
>   in April and May.
> - Confirm or correct: **peregrine falcons** nesting on the East Rock trap-rock
>   cliffs, **harbour seals** in the lower river and harbour in winter, and
>   **Asian shore crab** abundance on these shorelines.
>
> For each species, give the sources, and flag anything where the popular claim
> outruns the evidence.
